Question 2: contingency (r x c) table

This table counts the cars in mtcars by number of gears (rows) and number of cylinders (columns). For example, 12 cars have 3 gears and 8 cylinders.

data(mtcars)
assignment9 <- table(mtcars$gear, mtcars$cyl, dnn = c("gears", "cylinders"))

2.1 Row and column totals with addmargins()

addmargins(assignment9)
Counts with row and column sums
gears \ cylinders468Sum
3121215
484012
52125
Sum1171432

There are 32 cars in total. By gears, 15 cars have 3 gears, 12 have 4 gears and 5 have 5 gears. By cylinders, 11 cars have 4 cylinders, 7 have 6 and 14 have 8.

2.2 Proportion of each cell with prop.table()

prop.table(assignment9)
Each count divided by the grand total (32)
gears \ cylinders468
30.031250.062500.37500
40.250000.125000.00000
50.062500.031250.06250

Every cell is divided by the grand total of 32, so the nine values add up to 1. The largest share belongs to 3-gear, 8-cylinder cars at 0.375, or 37.5% of the dataset.

2.3 Row proportions with margin = 1

prop.table(assignment9, margin = 1)
Each count divided by its row total
gears \ cylinders468
30.06666670.13333330.8000000
40.66666670.33333330.0000000
50.40000000.20000000.4000000

With margin = 1, each row is divided by its own row total, so every row adds up to 1. Among 3-gear cars, 80% have 8 cylinders. Among 4-gear cars, about 67% have 4 cylinders and none have 8. Among 5-gear cars, 40% have 4 cylinders, 20% have 6 and 40% have 8.
